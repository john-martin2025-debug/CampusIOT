Inefficient campus resource management

Electricity and water on most campuses are checked by hand, if at all. By the time someone notices a problem, the waste has already happened.

What goes wrong, and what it costs

ProblemResult
Electricity and water use are not monitored.Resources are wasted without anyone knowing.
Lights and AC run in unoccupied rooms.Operating costs go up.
Water leaks can go undetected for days.Higher environmental impact.
Manual monitoring is slow and inconsistent.Corrective action is delayed.
There is no central, real-time resource data.Decisions can't be based on data.

What we need is a system that monitors campus resources continuously, spots inefficient use, and gets the information to someone who can fix it in time.

Who it is for

Students

See how their block is doing and report a running tap or an empty lit room from their phone.

Faculty

Rooms power down on their own after class, and problems can be flagged without filling in paperwork.

Facility managers

Get alerts with the exact location and estimated loss, so the worst problems get fixed first.

Administration

Get consumption records for budgeting and for environmental audits under ISO 14001.

IoT-based predictive resource allocation

We compared six approaches and chose Solution 4: a network of low-cost sensor nodes that watches occupancy, power and water flow in every monitored room and acts on what it finds.

Core objective

To deploy an IoT-based predictive resource allocation system that automates campus utility management, eliminates phantom electrical loads and water leaks, and reduces the institution's carbon footprint.

System scope

Distributed ESP32 microcontrollers, passive infrared (PIR) occupancy sensors and digital flow meters replace error-prone manual audits with consistent, automated environmental monitoring in line with ISO 14001.

How it works

  1. Sense

    A PIR sensor detects whether anyone is in the room. A current sensor measures the power drawn by lights, fans and AC. A flow meter on the water line measures litres per minute.

  2. Decide at the edge

    An ESP32 in each room reads the sensors every few seconds and checks for waste, such as power on with no motion for 10 minutes, or water flowing at 3 a.m.

  3. Send

    Readings travel over the campus Wi-Fi to a central server, which stores them as a time series for every room and zone.

  4. Analyse and predict

    The server compares each reading with that room's normal pattern, forecasts the day's usage, and flags anything unusual.

  5. Act

    A relay cuts power to empty rooms after a grace period. Leaks and faults are sent as alerts to the facility team with the location and estimated loss.

  6. Report

    The dashboard shows live and historical usage, and keeps records that support ISO 14001 audits.

Why we chose it

  • It has a simple, user-friendly design.
  • It uses precise sensor readings and calculations to give exact figures.
  • It reduces water and power waste by reporting issues as they happen.

The other options we compared

Solution 1: Centralized resource data platform

IoT sensors collect electricity, water, waste and occupancy data for selected facilities, and a web interface shows consumption patterns and unusual usage.

Solution 2: Sensor alerts to responsible staff

Smart sensors monitor electricity, water and classroom occupancy, and a web portal sends alerts by email, SMS and in-portal notification to faculty, staff and facility managers.

Solution 3: Timed load-shedding

Programmable digital timers and heavy-duty relays at the main distribution boards cut power at set times to stop after-hours energy waste.

Solution 4: IoT predictive resource allocation (selected)

ESP32 nodes, PIR occupancy sensors and digital flow meters automate utility management and remove phantom loads and leaks. This website is built around it.

Solution 5: Timetable-synced energy management

ESP32 nodes linked to the university timetable switch off HVAC and lighting in unused rooms and sleep idle lab PCs, with an Eco-Token reward system for students.

Solution 6: Campus resource-sharing portal

A central inventory and portal lets departments and students list, reserve and transfer underused equipment, furniture and lab materials.
